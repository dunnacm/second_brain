## B) Obsidian-ready delivery rules (math notes / formulas)
- When you ask for **paste-ready Obsidian content**, I return it inside a **single** fenced block:
  - ` ```markdown ... ``` `
- Display math uses **only** `$$ ... $$` (never `\[...\]`).
- **Never** use `\tfrac`; use `\frac` (and typically `\dfrac` when you want display-style fractions).
- Use `\Leftrightarrow`, and in aligned chains space as `\quad \Leftrightarrow \quad`.
- In tables containing absolute values, use `\lvert \cdot \rvert` (avoid `| |` in Markdown tables).

## C) Callouts policy (real analysis / theorem notes)
- Callouts are used **only** for:

- Theorem/definition callout template (exact structure):
  - Outer: `[!note]+ **Title**`

- Do **not** put full proofs or ordinary remarks inside callouts unless explicitly requested.

## D) Image → LaTeX (proof) transcription protocol (main workflow)

### D1) Output formatting
- Entire proof in: `$$\begin{aligned} ... \end{aligned}$$`
- **Every row begins with `&`** (including narrative rows).
- Equation labels are formatted as: `(n)\quad & ...` (label before the alignment tab).
- Narrative/section rows are their own rows, e.g.: `& \mathtt{Sub.\ (3),(4)\ into\ (1):}`
- **No two-ampersand alignment grids.** Default is a single alignment column; do not introduce extra `&` columns.

### D2) Alignment + indentation rules
- Use manual spacing (`\quad`, `\qquad`, `\,`) to visually align:
  - continuation `=` signs,
  - right-side parenthetical comments,
  - implication / biconditional markers.
- Derived implication lines must be **visibly nested**:
  - `& \Rightarrow (7)\quad ...`
  - `& \qquad\ \Rightarrow (8)\quad ...`
  - `& \qquad\ \qquad\ \Rightarrow (9)\quad ...`
- Continuation/evaluation lines that belong under a step should be indented to sit visually under the relevant content (especially under `=` or under a parent `\Rightarrow`).
- Spacing markers: default `\\[12pt]`, `\\[18pt]`, `\\[22pt]` (avoid larger gaps unless the source clearly demands it).

### D3) Kaizen (gold-standard matching)
- When you provide a “closer to what I needed” block, it becomes the formatting gold standard:
  - preserve step labels (including primes), phrasing, punctuation, spacer pattern,
  - do not restructure the proof,
  - change only what is necessary for correctness or what you explicitly request.

### D4) Mandatory math-coherence scan
Actively check and correct:
- radicands/signs stay consistent (e.g., `x^2-y^2=1` implies `\sqrt{x^2-1}`),
- differentiation steps match the prior expression,
- substitution matches the integrand,
- if the source image has a math mistake, **flag + correct** it (don’t silently propagate).

## E) \mathtt typography protocol (updated in this chat)
### E1) Principle: convention-bound vs typography-discretionary
- **Do NOT wrap convention-bound objects/fonts in `\mathtt{...}`**, including:
  - `\mathbb{R}`, `\mathbb{N}`, etc. (blackboard-bold sets),
  - `\mathcal{D}`, `\mathcal{R}` (domain/range notation),
  - the function space object `C(\mathbb{R})` (or `C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})`).
- **DO wrap typography-discretionary tokens in `\mathtt{...}`**, including:
  - function names like `sinh`, `cosh`, `tanh` (when you want the “name token” styled),
  - narrative/explanatory text (“Sub.”, “From…”, “Coordinates…”, etc.).

### E2) “No style spread”
- Apply `\mathtt` deliberately; do not let it “spread” onto nearby convention-bound symbols unless explicitly requested.

### E3) Function-property template (domain/range/continuity shorthand)
- Use the shorthand `f\in C(\mathbb{R})` (not `C\in\mathbb{R}`).
- If you want the function-name token in `\mathtt`, keep the rest conventional, e.g.
  - `\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{sinh}}=\mathbb{R}\ \bigl(= (-\infty,\infty)\bigr)`
  - `\mathtt{sinh}\in C(\mathbb{R})`

## J) Diagram refinement protocol (photo/scan → “PDF screenshot” look) (created/updated in this chat)
When you provide a photo/scan of a textbook diagram and want it to look like a clean PDF screenshot:
- **Geometric correction first:** remove warping/skew via perspective (projective) correction; enforce:
  - x-axis perfectly horizontal,
  - y-axis perfectly vertical (90° to x-axis),
  - all intended horizontal construction lines become horizontal,
  - all intended vertical construction lines become vertical.
- **Background normalization:** whiten paper background; remove grain/specks without erasing fine strokes.
- **Line clarity:** light sharpening after denoise; avoid thickening or “haloing” lines.
- **Shading cleanup:** even out shaded regions (remove blotchiness) while preserving crisp boundaries.
- **Crop + margins:** tight crop to content with clean margins (screenshot-like framing).
- **Output:** high-resolution PNG suitable for direct paste into Obsidian.
