## Double Angle Identity (Cosecant):
### $\boldsymbol{\csc(2x)=\dfrac{\sec x\,\csc x}{2}.}$
**Assumption:** $\sin x\neq 0$ and $\cos x\neq 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad & \csc(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\sin(2x)} \\[10pt]
(2)\quad & \csc(2x)=\dfrac{1}{2\sin x\cos x} \\[6pt]
& \qquad\ \qquad\ =\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{\cos x}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{\sin x}\right) \\[6pt]
& \qquad\ \qquad\ =\dfrac{\sec x\,\csc x}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\therefore\ \csc(2x)=\dfrac{\sec x\,\csc x}{2}.}
$$


### $\boldsymbol{\csc(2x)=\dfrac{1+\tan^2 x}{2\tan x}.}$
**Assumption:** $\tan x\neq 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad & \csc(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\sin(2x)} \\[10pt]
(2)\quad & \csc(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\dfrac{2\tan x}{1+\tan^2 x}} \\[8pt]
& \qquad\ \qquad\ =\dfrac{1+\tan^2 x}{2\tan x}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\therefore\ \csc(2x)=\dfrac{1+\tan^2 x}{2\tan x}.}
$$



## Double Angle Identity (Secant):
### $\boldsymbol{\sec(2x)=\dfrac{1+\tan^2 x}{1-\tan^2 x}.}$
**Assumption:** $\cos x\neq 0$ and $1-\tan^2 x\neq 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad & \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\cos(2x)} \\[10pt]
(2)\quad & \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\dfrac{1-\tan^2 x}{1+\tan^2 x}} \\[10pt]
& \qquad\ \qquad\ =\dfrac{1+\tan^2 x}{1-\tan^2 x}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\therefore\ \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1+\tan^2 x}{1-\tan^2 x}.}
$$


### $\boldsymbol{\sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{2\cos^2 x-1}.}$
**Assumption:** $2\cos^2 x-1\neq 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad & \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\cos(2x)} \\[10pt]
(2)\quad & \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{2\cos^2 x-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\therefore\ \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{2\cos^2 x-1}.}
$$


### $\boldsymbol{\sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{1-2\sin^2 x}.}$
**Assumption:** $1-2\sin^2 x\neq 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad & \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\cos(2x)} \\[10pt]
(2)\quad & \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{1-2\sin^2 x}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\therefore\ \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{1-2\sin^2 x}.}
$$


### $\boldsymbol{\sec(2x)=\dfrac{\sec^2 x}{2-\sec^2 x}.}$
**Assumption:** $\cos x\neq 0$ and $2-\sec^2 x\neq 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad & \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\cos(2x)} \\[10pt]
(2)\quad & \sec(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x-\sin^2 x} \\[6pt]
& \qquad\ \qquad\ =\dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x(1-\tan^2 x)} \\[6pt]
& \qquad\ \qquad\ =\dfrac{\sec^2 x}{1-\tan^2 x} \\[10pt]
(3)\quad & 1-\tan^2 x=1-(\sec^2 x-1)=2-\sec^2 x \\[10pt]
(4)\quad & \sec(2x)=\dfrac{\sec^2 x}{2-\sec^2 x}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\therefore\ \sec(2x)=\dfrac{\sec^2 x}{2-\sec^2 x}.}
$$



## Double Angle Identity (Cotangent):
### $\boldsymbol{\cot(2x)=\dfrac{1-\tan^2 x}{2\tan x}.}$
**Assumption:** $\tan x\neq 0$ and $1-\tan^2 x\neq 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad & \cot(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\tan(2x)} \\[10pt]
(2)\quad & \cot(2x)=\dfrac{1}{\dfrac{2\tan x}{1-\tan^2 x}} \\[6pt]
& \qquad\ \qquad\ =\dfrac{1-\tan^2 x}{2\tan x}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\therefore\ \cot(2x)=\dfrac{1-\tan^2 x}{2\tan x}.}
$$


### $\boldsymbol{\cot(2x)=\dfrac{\cot^2 x-1}{2\cot x}.}$
**Assumption:** $\cot x\neq 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad & \cot(2x)=\dfrac{1-\tan^2 x}{2\tan x} \\[10pt]
(2)\quad & \cot(2x)=\dfrac{1-\left(\dfrac{1}{\cot x}\right)^2}{2\left(\dfrac{1}{\cot x}\right)} \\[6pt]
& \qquad\ \qquad\ =\dfrac{\cot^2 x-1}{2\cot x}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\therefore\ \cot(2x)=\dfrac{\cot^2 x-1}{2\cot x}.}
$$
