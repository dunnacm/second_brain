## Identity:
### $\boldsymbol{\sec^2 x\,\csc^2 x=\sec^2 x+\csc^2 x.}$

**Assumption:** $\sin x\neq 0$ and $\cos x\neq 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad & \sec^2 x\,\csc^2 x
=\left(\dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{\sin^2 x}\right)
=\dfrac{1}{\sin^2 x\cos^2 x} \\[10pt]
(2)\quad & \sec^2 x+\csc^2 x
=\dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}+\dfrac{1}{\sin^2 x}
=\dfrac{\sin^2 x+\cos^2 x}{\sin^2 x\cos^2 x} \\[10pt]
(3)\quad & \sin^2 x+\cos^2 x=1 \\[10pt]
(4)\quad & \sec^2 x+\csc^2 x
=\dfrac{1}{\sin^2 x\cos^2 x}
=\sec^2 x\,\csc^2 x
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\therefore\ \sec^2 x\,\csc^2 x=\sec^2 x+\csc^2 x.}
$$
