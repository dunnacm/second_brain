$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text{Assumption: } \sqrt{2}\in \mathbb{Q} \ \Rightarrow\ \sqrt{2}=\dfrac{m}{n}, \ \text{where } m\in\mathbb{Z}\ \wedge\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad 
& (\sqrt{2})^{2}=\left(\dfrac{m}{n}\right)^{2}
\\[6pt]
\quad &  2=\dfrac{m^{2}}{n^{2}}
\\[6pt]
\quad & 2\cdot n^{2}=m^{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
From eq. (1):
$$
\begin{aligned}
(2)\quad 
& m^{2}=2k,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}
\qquad (\text{i.e., } m^{2} \text{ is an even number})
\end{aligned}
$$
Sub. (2), (3) into eq. (1):
$$
\begin{aligned}
(3)\quad 
& 2\cdot n^{2}=(2\cdot k)^{2}
\\[6pt]
\quad & 2\cdot n^{2}=4\cdot k^{2}
\\[6pt]
\quad & n^{2}=2\cdot k^{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Sub eqs. (2), (3) into (1):
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1')\quad 
& 2\cdot n^{2}=m^{2}
\\[6pt]
\quad & 2\cdot(2k^{2})=2k
\\[6pt]
\quad &  4k^{2}=2k
\\[6pt]
\quad & 2k=0
\\[6pt]
\quad &  k=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
From eqs. (3), (1')
$$
\begin{aligned}
(4)\quad 
& \quad k=0 \ \Rightarrow\ n^{2}=0 \ \Rightarrow\ n=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Eq. (4) contradicts the assumption’s consequent; therefore, by *reductio ad absurdum*, its antecedent is **false**, namely $\sqrt{2}\notin\mathbb{Q}$.
