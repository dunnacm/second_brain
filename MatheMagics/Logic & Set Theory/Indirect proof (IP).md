## Fundamentals:

When direct proof is either **too difficult** or not **appropriate**, the "**indirect proof**", is a common approach. Also known as:

- Proof by contradiction
- Reductio ad absurdum

This inference rules can also be used to prove propositional forms that are not implications.

## Definition:

If a proposition (in propositional form) gives rise to a contradiction, its negation can be inferred.

## Use:

To use indirect proof, assume each premise and assume the negation of the conclusion. Then, proceed with the proof until a contradiction is reached (since IP involves proving an implication, the formal proof takes the same form as a proof involving DP). At this point deduce the original conclusion. Indirect proof can also be nested within another indirect subproof. As with direct proof one cannot appeal to lines within a subproof from outside of it

## [[Common names (indirect proof)]]:
- Proof by contradiction
- Reduction ad absurdum
