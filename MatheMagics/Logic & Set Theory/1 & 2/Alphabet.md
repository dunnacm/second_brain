## Definition:
Characters from a given collection of symbols.
A finite sequence of symbols drawn from the alphabet is called a **string**
## Principles:
### Alphabet and different subjects:
**Different subjects use different "vocabularies" of symbols to write those propositions.**

The *logical scaffolding* is broadly the same across subjects (words like "and," "or," "not," "if...then," "for all," "there exists"). But the *mathematical content* depends on the subject, so you use subject-specific symbols:

- Number theory: symbols like $0, 1, +, \cdot, \mid$ (divides), maybe $<$.
- Algebra/group theory: a binary operation symbol (often written $\cdot$ or $*$), an identity $e$, inverses ${}^{-1}$.
- Real analysis: symbols like $<, +$, absolute value $\lvert \cdot \rvert$, limits, etc.

**All mathematical conclusions can be expressed as propositions**, and the _only reason they look different across subjects_ is that each subject brings its own stock of non-logical symbols to talk about its objects and operations.
