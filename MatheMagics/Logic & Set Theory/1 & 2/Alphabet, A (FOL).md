## Fundamentals:

### Definition:

A **first-order alphabet** is the collection of symbols used to build the expressions of a first-order language. It consists of:
- **Logic symbols** (fixed “structural” symbols), and
- (optionally) **Theory symbols** (the “subject-matter” vocabulary).

It is denoted by **A**.

## Logic symbols vs Theory symbols 

### Theory symbols:

It is not necessary to choose any theory symbols. If there are any, however, two important points to keep in mind:

- Theory symbols cannot be the same symbols used for:
	◦ Connectives
	◦ Quantifiers
	◦ Equality
	◦ Grouping

- The selection of **theory symbols** has *precedence* over the selection of **variable symbols** (logic symbols). These two collections must have no common symbols. 

### Logic symbols:

It is assumed that the **logic symbols** —that are not *variable symbols*— **will be the same across all theories**  (models, subjects or fields of mathematics) where logic is used
