## Definition
**Theory symbols** (often called **nonlogical symbols**) are the symbols of a logical language whose meaning is **not fixed by logic itself**, but is instead supplied by the **interpretation/structure** (model) appropriate to the subject being studied (e.g., set theory, number theory, field theory).

If **logic symbols** provide the fixed **form** of expressions, then **theory symbols** provide the variable **content** (chosen to fit a particular theory).

## Role
Theory symbols supply the **content-specific vocabulary** of a theory.  
Logic symbols tell you how to build well-formed expressions; theory symbols tell you what those expressions are *about*.

## Types
Which theory symbols you have depends on the logical language.

- **Propositional logic (PL):**  
  O’Leary does not typically use the label “theory symbols” for PL. The nonlogical vocabulary role is played by **propositional variables** (sentence letters) such as $\mathtt{p},\mathtt{q},\mathtt{r},\dots$.

- **First-order logic (FOL):**  
  The theory symbols are the **nonlogical symbols** of the first-order language, typically including:
	- **constant symbols** $c,d,\dots$
	- **function symbols** $f,g,\dots$ (with arity)
	- **predicate/relation symbols** $P,Q,\dots$ (with arity)
