## Definition:
The *arity* of a predicate (or relation symbol) is the number of arguments (terms) it takes. In FOL, each predicate symbol **P** is assigned a natural number *n*, called its **arity**, which specifies how many terms must follow it to form an *atomic formula*. 
The arity of a predicate tells one **how many arguments** it requires. **[[Unary predicates]]** express properties, while **[[Higher-arity predicates]]** express relations among multiple objects.

## Notation:
If **P** is an *n*-ary predicate symbol, then $P(t_0, \, t_1​, \, t_2​, \, . \, . \, . \,, \, t_n​)$ is an atomic formula whenever each $t_i$ is a term.

## Naming convention:

**1 → unary**
**2 → binary**
**3 → ternary**
**4 → quaternary**
**5 → quinary**
**6 → senary** (sometimes _hexary_)
**7 → septenary**
**8 → octonary**
**9 → nonary**
**10 → denary**

### Examples:
- Unary predicate: Even(*x*) (property of a single object)
- Binary predicate: Less(x,y) (relation between two objects)
- Ternary predicate: Between(x,y,z) (relation between three objects)
