## Assignment operator (`:=`)

The symbol `:=` is the **assignment operator** (often read **"is assigned to be"** or **"let … be defined as …"**).

### What it does

It introduces a **new symbol/name** and stipulates its meaning by **definition**.

- Example (defining a predicate):
  $$\mathtt{G}(\mathtt{x}) := \text{"}\mathtt{x}\text{ is a geometer"}$$

- Example (defining a formula abbreviation):
  $$\varphi := (\mathtt{P} \to \mathtt{Q}) \land (\mathtt{R} \leftrightarrow \neg \mathtt{P})$$

### Important distinction

- `:=` is **not** the same as "="
- "=" states **equality** between two already-meaningful expressions
- `:=` **creates/declares** meaning (a definition or assignment)
