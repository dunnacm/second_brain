# Linguistics

## Purpose

I want a better hold on language: to understand, remember, and use the words and expressions I encounter in books, television, and films I enjoy. The author's or speaker's context is the starting point. From one encounter, I want to explore a verified family of related words without making the project difficult to maintain or read.

Keep the notes concise, consistently arranged, and easy to revisit through their sources and word-family links. There is no separate active-review list, learning-status property, scheduled review routine, or automatic recall exercise.

## Source structure

- **Authors → author → work → chapter or passage.** The current author is C.S. Lewis, with *The Screwtape Letters* and *Screwtape Proposes a Toast* as sibling works.
- **Audio-Visual Media → T.V. → universe, where useful → series → episode or scene.** The current series is *A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms*, under GOT Universe.
- **Audio-Visual Media → Movies → film → scene, when needed.**

Create chapter, episode, and scene pages only when there is material to collect. Use specific titles such as `The Screwtape Letters - Chapter 1` to avoid ambiguous links. Keep tags lowercase and consistent with the source hierarchy. Word pages use `linguistics/vocabulary`; source links carry their provenance without requiring a new tag for every encounter.

## One page per word or expression

Keep one canonical page for each distinct word or expression in this project. Search existing notes before creating a page. If the same word occurs in several works, reuse its page and append the new encounter. Use a project-qualified wikilink if another vault note has the same title.

Each page should have these short sections:

1. **Meaning in context:** part of speech, a concise definition in original wording, and the sense relevant to the encounter. Add other senses when they help explain actual use.
2. **Encounters:** the source-note link, a supplied or verified excerpt, and its chapter/page or episode/timestamp when known. Explain any important nuance. Preserve supplied wording and mark missing locations explicitly.
3. **Examples:** at least one original sentence labelled as an original example, plus a short attested usage with a citation when one can be verified. Never present an invented sentence as dialogue from a book, film, or series. For a derived word with no encounter yet, say so; record a missing attestation rather than inventing one.
4. **Word family and expressions:** verified wikilinks, grouped by relationship. Distinguish derivatives, compounds, expressions, synonyms, and historical relatives. Cite evidence for membership and any usage label such as archaic, dialectal, or technical.
5. **Sources:** dictionary entry links and usage sources, with the date checked.

Use `tags` and `down` as the basic properties. Keep definitions, part of speech, source details, and usage labels in the body. Do not add empty metadata fields by default.

## Word-family scope

Merriam-Webster is the primary dictionary. Inspect the main entry, its listed derivatives, relevant cross-references, and browse listings. A browse page supplies candidates; alphabetical proximity or a shared spelling does not establish family membership. Verify compounds and forms with changed spelling as well as obvious suffixes.

For example, the supplied [Merriam-Webster browse page](https://www.merriam-webster.com/browse/dictionary/g/34) lists grief, griefful, grieffully, griefless, grieflessness, and grief-stricken. The [griefful entry](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/griefful) includes grieffully, and the [griefless entry](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/griefless) includes grieflessness. These illustrate the requested expansion; they are not a claim that this is the complete family.

**Exhaustive means exhaustive within the declared source and relationship scope.** Record which entries and listings were checked and disclose inaccessible entries or unresolved candidates. Name any supplementary dictionary and explain why it was needed. Never manufacture a plausible-looking form to complete a pattern, or claim completeness while relevant evidence remains unchecked.

Create a page for every verified distinct lexical form in the agreed family, including separately listed derivative adverbs and nouns. As a starting convention, ordinary inflections remain listed within their base entry unless they have a distinct meaning or use; they are still included in the inventory. Do not recursively expand every synonym into another full family. This keeps the expansion tied to the supplied word while retaining its verified relatives.

## Links and relationship meaning

Use `down` for intentional navigation: source category → work → passage or episode → encountered word → selected family expansion. A family branch means “explore this verified relative”; it must not falsely assert that every member derives directly from the anchor word.

Link the source note to the encountered word. Put return citations in the word's Encounters section and classify them as references during mapping. Synonyms, comparisons, and provenance links are also references unless their context explicitly establishes another relationship. Reciprocal family membership need not be duplicated in `down` on every page; explain it in the body and classify its meaning when mapping.

Examples written as literal wikilink syntax in code are instructions, not actual project relationships. Keep the project-description and protocol prose distinct from vocabulary content when extracting the map.
